Model and assumptions

Research question: How does a constant GTG setpoint change storage utilization, and which setpoints remain within power and SoC limits?

Energy balance: PGTG + Pdis = Pload + Pch.
SoCk+1 = SoCk + (ηchPch − Pdis/ηdis) Δt / E, with Δt in hours.

  • P4 basis: power balancing and SoC equation (§2.3.3, Eq. 16), four metrics and setpoint sweep (§2.4). This is not the authors’ code, trace or weighted optimizer.
  • Independent demand model: 48 asynchronous nodes switch among training, fine-tuning and inference. Assumed dwell times: 600 / 180 / 30 seconds; relative activity: 1 / 0.75 / 0.35; bounded correlated fluctuations with 30-second correlation. Phase-selection weights use time share divided by dwell time (P4 Eq. 15).
  • Raw auxiliary demand is 0.4 MW + 25% of IT demand. The entire trace is rescaled to the requested facility mean. At fixed mean, phase shares change variability rather than average energy. These are illustrative assumptions, not measured AI telemetry.
  • GTG output is constant; no ramp, fuel, voltage/frequency, outage or degradation model. The displayed SoC is an unclipped diagnostic trajectory. After an equipment or physical energy limit is crossed, the assumed balance is not implementable.
  • Normal SoC limits are 20–90%, with an 80% reference. Meeting these limits alone does not demonstrate 12-hour outage autonomy. Efficiencies of 95% each way are assumptions.
  • Transitions count changes between charging and discharging, ignoring idle samples; they are not rainflow cycles or measured battery wear. Average SoC is the mean of interval-start states. Energy movement is charge + discharge measured on the AC side.
  • Metrics use every timestep. Plot lines preserve binned extrema when the trace is dense; hover shows a sampled point. The sweep has no automatically declared optimum: a decision needs a defined objective and feasibility constraints.
